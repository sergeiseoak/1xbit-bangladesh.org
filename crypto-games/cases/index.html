How Cases Payouts Work

Cases uses a random result within the prize range assigned to the selected case.

For example, imagine a case costs 10 units and can award between 1 and 40 units. A result of 6 units still counts as a prize, but it represents a net loss of 4 units. A result of 25 units would produce a net gain of 15 units.

These numbers are an illustration of the calculation, not a confirmed 1xBit payout table.

The key distinction is between receiving a prize and making a profit. Published rules describe Cases as awarding a cash amount when a case is opened, but that amount may be smaller than the stake.

The net result is calculated as:

Net result = Prize received − Total round cost

Any additional payment selected before opening the case must be included in the total cost.

What Do the 10%, 20% and 30% Options Do?

Descriptions of the 1xGames version include optional increases of 10%, 20% and 30% above the case's standard price.

These options are associated with improved potential payouts or chances of higher rewards. However, the exact mathematical effect needs to be checked in the version being played.

Paying extra doesn't guarantee that the final prize will exceed the total stake.

For a case with a base cost of 100 units, the total price would be 110, 120 or 130 units when those increases are applied. The actual result remains random.
