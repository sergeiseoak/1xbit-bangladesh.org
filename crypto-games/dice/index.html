How to Play Crypto Dice

Most crypto dice games use a numerical scale and let the player choose which side of a target the result should fall on.

A typical round works as follows:

Open Dice in the casino games catalogue.

Enter the amount to stake.

Choose Roll Under or Roll Over, if both options are available.

Adjust the target number or desired win probability.

Check the payout multiplier shown on screen.

Confirm the bet and view the generated result.

If the result falls within the selected winning range, the bet pays at the displayed multiplier. Otherwise, the stake is lost.

Roll Under vs Roll Over

In a Roll Under bet, the winning result must be below the selected threshold. In a Roll Over bet, it must be above the threshold.

For example, on a hypothetical 0–100 scale, a Roll Under target of 40 would cover approximately 40% of the range. A Roll Over target of 80 would cover approximately 20%.

The precise comparison operators and treatment of boundary values depend on the game's numerical system. A game using integers from 0 to 99 may calculate its outcomes differently from one using decimal values.

The fair multiplier is calculated as:

A casino typically applies a house edge, so the multiplier offered in a real game will generally be lower than the fair value.

Example: A 40% Win Chance

Suppose a player selects a 40% chance of winning and stakes 100 units.

Without a house edge, the fair total payout would be 250 units. If the game instead offers x2.45, the winning return would be 245 units, including the original stake.

That difference reflects the payout structure. The exact house edge cannot be established without knowing the real game's probability and multiplier settings.

What Is the House Edge in Dice?

The house edge is the mathematical advantage built into the payout system.

For a game with a 40% win probability and a x2.45 total payout multiplier:

Theoretical RTP = 0.40 × 2.45 × 100 = 98%

That corresponds to a theoretical house edge of 2%.

This is an illustrative calculation, not an RTP claim for Dice at 1xBit.

The formula is useful because it allows players to compare the probability displayed by a game with the multiplier actually offered. If both values are known, the theoretical return can be calculated directly.

Is Dice Provably Fair?

Many crypto-native dice games use provably fair systems. These allow a completed result to be checked using cryptographic data, often involving a server seed, client seed and nonce.

The typical verification process involves:

A cryptographic commitment to the server seed before play.

A client seed used as part of result generation.

A nonce identifying the round.

A revealed server seed and verification method after the relevant seed cycle.

The exact algorithm varies by developer. A game shouldn't be described as provably fair unless its interface provides the necessary verification information.

Provably fair verification helps establish that a result was generated according to the stated process. It doesn't predict the next roll or eliminate the house edge.

Dice Betting Strategies: What Actually Changes?

Players sometimes adjust the target after a losing round or increase their stake to recover previous losses.

Neither action changes the underlying probability of an independent random result.

A 20% winning chance remains 20% on the next round, regardless of how many previous bets have lost. Raising the stake increases the potential payout, but also increases the amount that can be lost.

Some dice interfaces offer automated betting options. If available, those controls can repeat bets or change stakes according to predefined settings. They do not turn a negative-expectation game into a profitable one.
