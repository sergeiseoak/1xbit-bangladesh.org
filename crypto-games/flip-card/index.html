How to Play Flip Card

The documented game follows a straightforward sequence:

Select the stake for the round.

Start the game to display six face-down cards.

Choose four cards to reveal.

Look for three cards showing identical payout odds.

Use a Joker as part of a matching combination if one appears.

Check the final result and the payout displayed by the game.

The winning combination must be found among the four selected cards. A matching trio elsewhere in the six-card layout does not help if those cards weren't revealed.

How the Four-Card Selection Works

Imagine the six hidden cards include three cards with matching odds. Revealing all three within your four selections completes the required combination.

If you uncover only two matching cards and the remaining selections show different values, you may not have a winning set unless one of those cards is a Joker.

The selection matters because you cannot see the card values beforehand. Choosing a particular position doesn't guarantee that a matching card is underneath it.

Can Every Round Produce a Winning Combination?

Descriptions of the game indicate that the full set of six cards contains a possible matching combination, either three cards with identical odds or two matching cards plus a Joker.

That doesn't mean every selection of four cards will reveal it.

The distinction is important: the winning combination may exist among the six hidden cards, but the player must uncover the necessary cards within the permitted selections.

This is why the game remains chance-based even though the player chooses which positions to reveal.

Does Choosing Different Cards Improve Your Chances?

The cards are hidden before selection, so their positions don't provide a reliable way to identify the winning combination.

A previous round also doesn't establish where a Joker or matching multiplier will appear next.

Choosing the same four positions repeatedly isn't a proven strategy, and switching positions after a loss doesn't make the next round more likely to win.
