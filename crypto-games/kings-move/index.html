How the Chessboard Works

King's Move begins on a 3×3 board. With the king in the centre, eight surrounding squares are available for the opening selection.

After the king moves, the board expands to 5×5. Three knights then create the threat.

In ordinary chess, a knight moves in an L-shaped pattern: two squares in one direction and one square perpendicular to it. A knight can jump over other pieces.

The game uses this familiar chess movement to determine threatened positions. However, King's Move does not reproduce every rule of a conventional chess match.
