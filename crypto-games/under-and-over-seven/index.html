The Under and Over bets have identical mathematical probabilities. Exactly 7 occurs less often, which explains its higher payout multiplier.

Under 7

An Under bet wins when the dice add up to 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6.

The lowest possible total is 2, when both dice show 1. The highest winning total for Under is 6.

Over 7

An Over bet wins when the combined total is 8, 9, 10, 11 or 12.

A total of exactly 7 does not count as an Over result.

Exactly 7

This option wins only when the dice total is 7.

Six combinations produce that result: 1+6, 2+5, 3+4, 4+3, 5+2 and 6+1.

That makes seven the most common individual total, although it is still less likely than the combined group of Under or Over outcomes.

What Are the Actual Chances of Winning?

Two fair six-sided dice have 36 equally likely ordered combinations. Those combinations are not distributed evenly across totals: there is only one way to roll 2, but six ways to roll 7.

Under and Over 7 RTP and House Edge

The published multipliers also allow a theoretical return calculation.

For Under and Over:

15 ÷ 36 × 2.3 = 95.83% theoretical RTP

For Exactly 7:

6 ÷ 36 × 5.8 = 96.67% theoretical RTP

These figures are calculated from the published multipliers and the probabilities of two fair dice. They are not independently verified RTP specifications for a 1xBit implementation.

The corresponding theoretical house edges are approximately 4.17% for Under or Over and 3.33% for Exactly 7.

A higher theoretical return does not guarantee a win in an individual round. Exactly 7 still loses on 30 of the 36 possible dice combinations.

Can You Predict the Next Dice Roll?

No reliable betting pattern can predict the next result in a properly random dice game.

Five consecutive Over results do not make Under more likely on the sixth roll. Each new round has the same mathematical probabilities, assuming the dice are fair and the results independent.

Previous results can be useful for checking completed rounds, but they do not change the probabilities of future throws.

Likewise, increasing a stake after a loss does not improve the chance of winning. It only changes the amount at risk.
